Anne Arundel County Healthcare & Hospitals

Anne Arundel County Healthcare & Hospitals

Anne Arundel County has excellent healthcare facilities, from major hospitals to specialized clinics. This guide covers where to go for different medical needs.

Emergency Services

When to Use Emergency Room

Use 911 or go to ER for:

  • Chest pain or difficulty breathing
  • Severe injuries
  • Stroke symptoms
  • Severe bleeding
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Poisoning or overdose
  • Serious burns
  • Broken bones with deformity

Use Urgent Care for:

  • Minor injuries
  • Fever and illness
  • Minor cuts requiring stitches
  • Urinary tract infections
  • Minor fractures
  • Rashes
  • Eye infections

Emergency Room Wait Times

Vary by time and day:

  • Weekday evenings typically busiest
  • Weekends can have longer waits
  • Major trauma cases priority
  • Ask about expected wait when arriving

All Three Anne Arundel Hospitals

AAMC: Annapolis - main county hospital
Holy Cross: Glen Burnie - full service
MedStar: Multiple clinic locations - primary and specialty care

Primary Care Physicians

Finding a Doctor

Insurance First:

  • Check your insurance provider network
  • Use insurer’s “find a doctor” tool
  • Verify doctor accepts your plan
  • Call office to confirm accepting new patients

Questions to Ask:

  • Do you accept my insurance?
  • Are you accepting new patients?
  • How long until first appointment?
  • Where is office located?
  • Office hours that work for you?
  • How to reach doctor after hours?

Pediatric Care

Children’s Healthcare Providers:

  • AAC Pediatric Associates
  • Children’s National Hospital affiliated clinics
  • Individual pediatric practices
  • AACPS school-based health centers

Finding Pediatrician:

  • Ask for referrals from parents
  • Check insurance network
  • Visit office before choosing
  • Ask about after-hours care
  • Confirm hospital privileges

Options:

  • Private practices throughout county
  • Dental clinics at schools
  • Community health centers
  • University dental programs

  • Medicaid covers dental for children

When to Seek Emergency Care:

  • Knocked-out tooth

  • Uncontrolled bleeding
  • Infection with swelling

  • Dental schools may offer emergency care

Medicaid in Maryland

Eligibility:

  • Income-based
  • Disability status
  • Family status
  • Apply through Maryland Health Connection

Benefits:

  • Comprehensive coverage
  • Low or no premium
  • Covers doctor visits, hospital, prescriptions

Federal Program:

  • For age 65+
  • Some younger with disabilities
  • Multiple plan options
  • Supplemental insurance available

  • Family coverage available
  • Pre-tax premiums
  • Varies by employer

  • Compare plans and costs

Preventive Care

Annual Checkups

Adults:

  • Yearly physical exam
  • Blood pressure check
  • Cholesterol screening
  • Age-appropriate cancer screenings

Children:

  • Well-child visits schedule
  • Immunizations on schedule
  • Growth and development monitoring
  • School physicals

Immunizations

Recommended Schedule:

  • Children have specific schedule
  • Adults need boosters
  • Travel vaccines if applicable
  • Flu shot annually

Where to Get Vaccines:

  • Doctor offices
  • Pharmacies
  • Health departments
  • Some walk-in clinics

Screenings

Age-Dependent:

  • Mammograms (women 40+)
  • Colon cancer screening (adults 45-50+)
  • Prostate screening (men 50+)
  • Eye exams regularly
  • Dental cleanings twice yearly

Health Records

Getting Medical Records

From Doctor Offices:

  • Request in writing
  • May have fee for copies
  • Can request electronic copies
  • Usually within 30 days

From Hospitals:

  • Medical records department
  • More extensive records
  • May take longer to process
  • Photo ID required

Sharing Records

When Moving:

  • Request transfer to new doctor
  • Provide new doctor’s info
  • Some offices handle transfer
  • Keep copies for yourself

Tips for Healthcare in Anne Arundel

  1. Know your insurance - Understand coverage before needing care
  2. Keep records - Maintain personal health file
  3. Find a PCP - Primary care doctor for routine care
  4. Know emergency vs urgent - Don’t ER for minor issues
  5. Ask about costs - Get estimates for procedures
  6. Use preventive care - Stay up to date on screenings
  7. Keep pharmacy consistent - Helps with drug interaction monitoring
  8. Update insurance info - Keep current with changes
