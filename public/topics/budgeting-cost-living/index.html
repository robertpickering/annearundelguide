Budgeting for Anne Arundel County
#

Estimated reading time: 6 minutes

Moving to Anne Arundel County means understanding the local costs. Some are obvious (rent or mortgage), but others sneak up on you (property taxes, utility seasonal variations). Here’s what’s known about budgeting for life in the county.

Housing Costs: The Big Ticket
#

Housing is typically 30-40% of your budget, and Anne Arundel County runs above the national average.

Rent
#

One-bedroom apartments:

  • Annapolis: $1,400-1,800/month
  • Glen Burnie: $1,100-1,400/month
  • Severna Park: $1,300-1,600/month
  • Crofton: $1,200-1,500/month

Two-bedroom units:

  • Annapolis: $1,800-2,400/month
  • Glen Burnie: $1,400-1,800/month
  • Severna Park: $1,600-2,200/month
  • Crofton: $1,500-2,000/month

Three-bedroom homes:

  • Range from $2,200-3,500/month depending on location and condition

Tip: Rent prices vary significantly by neighborhood, not just by town. A house in the “good school district” area can cost $500-1,000 more per month than the same house two streets over.

Home Prices
#

Median home prices (2024-2025):

  • Annapolis: $550,000-700,000
  • Glen Burnie: $350,000-450,000
  • Severna Park: $500,000-650,000
  • Crofton: $400,000-550,000
  • Odenton: $375,000-500,000

Down payment: Most lenders expect 20%, though FHA loans allow 3.5% down. VA loans require no down payment for eligible veterans.

Property Taxes
#

Anne Arundel County property taxes are about $1.00 per $100 of assessed value.

Example calculations:

  • $400,000 home: ~$4,000-4,400/year in property taxes
  • $500,000 home: ~$5,000-5,500/year
  • $600,000 home: ~$6,000-6,600/year

The catch: The assessed value is typically 10% of market value, so a $500,000 home might be assessed at only $50,000. The tax calculation uses the assessed value, not the market value.

Utilities: What You Actually Pay
#

Electricity (AEP Maryland)
#

Average monthly bills:

  • Apartment (1BR): $80-120/month
  • Townhouse (2-3BR): $100-150/month
  • Single family (3-4BR): $150-250/month

Seasonal variation: Winter and summer months are higher due to heating and cooling. Expect 20-30% higher bills in extreme weather months.

Water and Sewer
#

Average monthly:

  • Apartments (if not included): $30-50/month
  • Townhouses: $60-100/month (usually combined)
  • Single family: $80-150/month

Water rates are charged by usage, typically around $4-6 per 1,000 gallons. Most households use 5,000-10,000 gallons per month.

Natural Gas (if applicable)
#

Average monthly bills:

  • Townhouse: $60-120/month in winter
  • Single family: $100-200/month in winter
  • Summer: Minimal, typically $15-30/month

Not all homes in the county have natural gas - some use electric heating or heat pumps.

Internet
#

Monthly costs:

  • Basic cable (50-100 Mbps): $50-70/month
  • Mid-tier (200-400 Mbps): $60-90/month
  • High-speed (600+ Mbps): $80-120/month

Promotions: Many providers offer 12 months at a discounted rate, then rates increase. Always ask about the regular rate after any promotion ends.

Groceries and Food
#

Average monthly grocery spending:

  • Single person: $300-450/month
  • Couple: $450-750/month
  • Family of 4: $700-1,200/month

Cost factors: Where you shop makes a big difference. Aldi and Walmart are significantly cheaper than Whole Foods or specialty stores. Meal planning and buying generic brands can save 20-30% on your grocery bill.

Transportation
#

Car Costs
#

Gas: For a typical commute (20 miles/day, 20 days/month), expect $150-250/month depending on gas prices and your vehicle’s efficiency.

Parking: Most employers provide free parking. If not, expect $100-300/month for dedicated parking.

Insurance: Varies widely by age, driving record, and vehicle. Expect $100-200/month for most drivers.

Maintenance: Budget $50-100/month on average for oil changes, tires, and unexpected repairs.

Total transportation: $350-600/month for most people with a car.

Public Transit
#

If you use public transit regularly:

  • Monthly MTA pass: $75/month
  • MARC train: $150-250/month (for DC commuters)

Income Requirements
#

To live comfortably in Anne Arundel County:

Single person:

  • Needs: $45,000-55,000/year
  • Comfortable: $65,000-80,000/year
  • Upper income: $100,000+/year

Family of 4:

  • Needs: $75,000-90,000/year
  • Comfortable: $110,000-140,000/year
  • Upper income: $175,000+/year

These are rough estimates and depend heavily on your housing choices and lifestyle preferences.

Budgeting Tips
#

  1. Start with housing: This is your biggest expense. Choose wisely based on what you can afford long-term.

  2. Factor in seasonality: Utilities, heating/cooling, and even grocery costs vary by season. Build in a buffer for winter and summer months.

  3. Research taxes: Property taxes are an annual expense that can catch you off guard if you’re not expecting it.

  4. Build an emergency fund: Aim for 3-6 months of expenses before major purchases.

  5. Track spending: For the first few months, track every dollar. You’ll discover patterns and waste you didn’t know you had.

Our Experience
#

Living here for over 10 years, budgeting is more about expectations than restrictions. The cost of living is higher than the national average, but it’s manageable if you know what you’re budgeting for.

The biggest surprise for most new residents is property taxes - they’re higher than in many other areas, and they add up quickly. Also, utility costs can be higher in winter if you’re not used to Maryland’s cold snaps.

But when you factor in the good schools, the access to both Baltimore and DC, and the overall quality of life, most people find it’s worth the extra cost.
