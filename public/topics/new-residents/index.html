New Residents Guide: Everything You Need to Know About Moving to Anne Arundel County

Complete guide for new residents moving to Anne Arundel County, Maryland. Learn about vehicle registration, utilities, healthcare, schools, and settling in smoothly.

New Residents Guide: Everything You Need to Know About Moving to Anne Arundel County

Estimated reading time: 6 minutes

Moving to a new place means starting fresh—but it also means figuring out a lot of things you never thought about until you actually need them. As someone who has helped multiple families navigate the move to Anne Arundel County, I’ve learned what actually matters in those crucial first weeks. Let’s walk through what you need to know.

Understanding Your First Month

The first month in Anne Arundel County isn’t about checking every box perfectly. It’s about getting the essentials sorted and then building your life from there. Here’s what to focus on:

Registration and Documentation

Moving to Maryland from another state means you need to update a few things on your paperwork:

Driver’s License and Vehicle Registration

Maryland law requires you to get a Maryland driver’s license and register your vehicle within 30 days of establishing residency. This isn’t something to procrastinate on.

The Maryland Motor Vehicle Administration (MVA) has locations throughout the county. The Glen Burnie office tends to have shorter wait times compared to the downtown Annapolis location. Show up right when they open at 8:00 AM if you want to minimize your time in line.

You’ll need:

  • Your out-of-state driver’s license
  • Your vehicle title or current registration from your previous state
  • Proof of Maryland insurance
  • Two forms of identification
  • Payment for registration fees (typically $100-150 depending on your vehicle)

Important Note: If you’re renting, you’ll need a copy of your lease. If you own your home, bring your deed or closing documents.

Insurance Updates

Don’t forget to update your insurance policies. Maryland has specific requirements for auto insurance, and you need to notify your home and health insurance providers of your new address.

Auto Insurance: Maryland requires minimum coverage of:

  • $30,000 for bodily injury per person
  • $60,000 for bodily injury per accident
  • $25,000 for property damage
  • $50,000 for uninsured motorist coverage

Homeowners/Renters Insurance: Contact your provider to update your address. If you’re moving from a different state, you may need to shop for new coverage that meets Maryland’s requirements.

Address Changes

The United States Postal Service makes it easy to forward your mail. You can set up forwarding online at USPS.com or visit your local post office. Plan to set this up at least two weeks before your move so you don’t miss anything important.

Don’t forget to update your address with:

  • Banks and credit card companies
  • Employer (for payroll and tax purposes)
  • Subscription services
  • Insurance providers
  • Doctors and healthcare providers
  • Utility companies (if not transferring)

Setting Up Your Utilities

Some utilities transfer automatically when you change addresses; others require you to start new accounts. Here’s what to handle:

Electricity

Anne Arundel County residents typically get electricity from AEP Maryland (also known as Potomac Electric Power Company). Call 410-486-1234 or set up service online at aepmaryland.com.

If you’re moving into an apartment, confirm with your landlord whether electricity is included in your rent or if you need to set up your own account. Sometimes the landlord keeps the account active for the entire building.

Water and Sewer

Anne Arundel County handles water and sewer services through their Department of Public Works. If you own your home, call 410-222-3258 to get set up. If you’re renting, your landlord typically covers this, but confirm before moving in.

Internet and Cable

This is the one where timing really matters. Several providers serve Anne Arundel County:

  • Comcast/Xfinity: Extensive coverage throughout the county
  • Verizon Fios: Available in select areas, especially north county
  • Google Fiber: Expanding coverage in certain neighborhoods
  • Spectrum: Alternative cable provider in some areas

Call at least two weeks before you move. Installation appointments fill up quickly, especially on weekends. Ask about promotional rates and equipment rental fees when you call.

Gas

Many Anne Arundel County homes use natural gas for heating and cooking. Potomac Edison provides gas service to much of the county. Call 1-800-477-1004 to start or transfer service.

Finding Your Local Healthcare Provider

If you have insurance, you need a primary care doctor within your network. Anne Arundel Medical Center (AAMC) is the main hospital with locations throughout the county. The outpatient clinics are well-regarded, but getting an appointment can take time.

Start by calling the number on your insurance card and asking for primary care physicians in your ZIP code. Call at least three offices—the first one you call might have an opening in three months, but someone else might have something next week.

School Enrollment (If You Have Kids)

Even if you’re not starting school mid-year, this should be on your radar. Anne Arundel County Public Schools (AACPS) has enrollment centers that handle new student registration.

You’ll need:

  • Proof of residence (lease, deed, or utility bills in your name)
  • Birth certificates for your children
  • Immunization records
  • Previous school records (if transferring)
  • Parent/guardian identification

AACPS Enrollment Services

  • Website: www.aacps.org
  • Phone: 410-222-8000
  • Main Enrollment Center: 601 S. Paca Street, Baltimore, MD 21201

Some areas have more available spots than others. Severna Park and Glen Burnie areas tend to have more capacity compared to some of the more competitive neighborhoods.

Understanding Local Services

Trash and Recycling

Each area in Anne Arundel County has different pickup schedules. Check with your landlord (if renting) or the county for your specific route. The county provides online lookup tools where you can enter your address and get your schedule.

Emergency Services

Anne Arundel County has comprehensive emergency services. Dial 911 for emergencies. The county operates with multiple police precincts and fire departments serving different areas.

Building Your Local Community

This is where you actually start feeling like you belong. Find one or two places to become regular at—a coffee shop, a gym, a park. Consistency builds community.

The county has a robust recreation program with pools, tennis courts, and programs for all ages. Check the county website for summer programs if you have kids—they fill up fast.

Local Libraries

Anne Arundel County Public Library has multiple branches throughout the county. They offer:

  • Free internet access
  • Computer and technology assistance
  • Children’s programs and story time
  • Community meeting spaces
  • Book clubs and events

Main Library: 101 College Avenue, Annapolis, MD 21401 Website: www.aacpl.gov

Quick Reference: First 30 Days Checklist

Week 1:

  • Update driver’s license address (if staying in-state)
  • Register your vehicle with Maryland MVA
  • Set up utility accounts (electric, gas, water, internet)
  • Set up mail forwarding with USPS
  • Find a grocery store near your home

Week 2:

  • Update address with banks and credit cards
  • Find a primary care physician
  • Update insurance policies
  • Register to vote (optional, but recommended)
  • Join a local gym or fitness group

Week 3:

  • Enroll children in school (if applicable)
  • Find a pediatrician (if you have kids)
  • Locate nearest hospital and emergency services
  • Update employer address for tax purposes
  • Subscribe to local newspaper or community newsletter

Week 4:

  • Explore local parks and recreation facilities
  • Find local places of worship (if applicable)
  • Join community social media groups
  • Register for county recreation programs
  • Set up regular routines (grocery shopping, gym, etc.)

Common Challenges New Residents Face

Traffic and Commuting: Maryland traffic can be challenging, especially on I-95 and the Baltimore-Washington Parkway during rush hour. Plan your commute during actual rush hour before finalizing where to live if you work in DC or Baltimore.

Weather: Maryland gets seasonal weather—hot humid summers, cold winters, and occasional snow. Make sure your home heating and cooling systems are working properly before the seasons change.

Local Regulations: Each municipality in Anne Arundel County has its own rules about trash pickup, snow removal, parking restrictions, and property maintenance. Take time to understand the specific rules for your address.
