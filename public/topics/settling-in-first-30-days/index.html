Your First Month in Anne Arundel County: A Realistic Guide

Your First Month in Anne Arundel County: A Realistic Guide

Estimated reading time: 5 minutes

Moving to a new place is equal parts exciting and overwhelming. You’ve got boxes everywhere, and somehow the simplest tasks feel impossible when you’re standing in an empty kitchen wondering if you should call a plumber or the county office first. Let’s cut through the noise and focus on what actually matters in those crucial first 30 days.

Week One: The Basics That Actually Matter

Your Vehicle Needs to Be Legal (and That Means Registration)

If you’re driving in Maryland, you need a Maryland registration within 30 days. Yes, the grace period is 30 days, but let’s not test it. The Annapolis MVA office can feel like a rite of passage with its wait times, but it’s straightforward if you’re prepared.

What you need:

  • Your out-of-state title or registration
  • Proof of Maryland insurance
  • Your lease or deed if renting (otherwise, it’s not required)
  • A valid ID
  • Payment for the fee ($100-150 depending on your vehicle)

Pro tip: The Glen Burnie MVA tends to have shorter lines. Show up right when they open at 8:00 AM, and you might be in and out before lunch.

Getting Utilities in Your Name

Some services auto-transfer when a new person moves in; others don’t. Don’t assume anything.

Electric (AEP Maryland): Call 410-486-1234 or set up online. If you’re moving into an apartment, sometimes the landlord keeps the account active, but confirm this.

Water/Sewer: Anne Arundel County handles this through their Department of Public Works. If you’re renting, your landlord likely covers this. If you own, call 410-222-3258 to get set up.

Internet: This is the one where timing really matters. Comcast, Verizon Fios, and Google Fiber all serve different areas. Call at least two weeks before you move - the installation appointments fill up fast, especially on weekends.

The Grocery Store You’ll Actually Use

Don’t get fancy with your first food run. You need essentials, and you need them now.

Budget-friendly: Aldi in Severna Park or Glen Burnie has everything you need at decent prices. Walmart in Crofton and Glen Burnie works well for a one-stop shop.

Better quality, fair price: Giant Food has locations throughout the county. They run sales weekly, and the loyalty program is genuinely useful.

Quick convenience: Target in Glen Burnie and the Annapolis area has a solid grocery section if you forget something basic.

Week Two: Finding Your Rhythm

Your Local Healthcare Provider

If you have insurance, you need a primary care doctor within your network. Anne Arundel Medical Center (AAMC) is the main hospital, with locations throughout the county. The outpatient clinics are well-regarded, but getting an appointment can take a while.

Start by calling the number on your insurance card and asking for primary care physicians in your ZIP code. Call at least three offices - the first one you call might have an opening in three months, but someone else might have something next week.

School Enrollment (If You Have Kids)

Even if you’re not starting school mid-year, this should be on your radar. Anne Arundel County Public Schools (AACPS) has enrollment centers that handle new student registration. The Severna Park and Glen Burnie areas tend to have more available spots compared to some of the more competitive neighborhoods.

You’ll need:

  • Proof of residence (lease, deed, or utility bills)
  • Birth certificates
  • Immunization records
  • Previous school records (if transferring)

The Commute Reality Check

If you work in DC or Baltimore, you need to understand your commute options. The MARC train from Union Station (which requires getting to the BWI station first) runs to Union Station in DC, but schedules are limited. The MTA commuter bus routes run during peak hours only.

Take the route once during rush hour before you commit to living in a particular area. That 30-minute commute during a test drive can become 75 minutes at 7:30 AM.

Week Three: Building Local Connections

This is where you actually start feeling like you belong. Find one or two places to become regular at - a coffee shop, a gym, a park. Consistency builds community.

The county has a robust recreation program with pools, tennis courts, and programs for all ages. Check the county website for summer programs if you have kids - they fill up fast.

Week Four: Settling Into Normalcy

By now, you should have established routines. You know where to get gas, where to drop off packages, and which coffee shop barista remembers your order.

If you haven’t already, register to vote in Maryland. You’ll need to do this within a certain timeframe to participate in upcoming elections. The county board of elections office is in Annapolis, but most registration can be done online.

A Few Things Most People Forget

  • Trash pickup: Each area has different schedules. Check with your landlord or county for your specific route.
  • Snow: Maryland gets occasional snow. If you’re renting, clarify who shovels sidewalks. If you own, know your emergency contacts.
  • Local news: Subscribe to a local paper or follow county social media. You need to know when roads close or when there’s a flood advisory.
