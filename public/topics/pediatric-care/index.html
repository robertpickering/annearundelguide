Pediatric Care in Anne Arundel County
#

Finding good pediatric care is crucial for your child’s health and development. Anne Arundel County has many pediatric practices serving families throughout the county.

Finding a Pediatrician
#

When to Start Looking
#

During Pregnancy:

  • Interview pediatricians while pregnant
  • Many offices have enrollment forms ready
  • Establish relationship before baby arrives
  • Some offer prenatal classes

After Birth:

  • First visit typically 3-5 days after birth
  • Some offices see baby at hospital before discharge
  • Schedule before leaving hospital if possible

What to Look For
#

Qualifications:

  • Board certified pediatrician
  • AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics) member
  • Appropriate hospital privileges
  • Experience with your child’s needs

Practical Considerations:

  • Office location convenient for you
  • Office hours that work with your schedule
  • Wait time for appointments
  • After-hours care availability
  • Phone responsiveness

Office Environment:

  • Child-friendly space
  • Clean and organized
  • Staff friendly with children
  • Equipment for infants and children

Questions to Ask Offices
#

Schedule:

  • How soon for well-child visits?
  • Same-day sick visits?
  • After-hours phone coverage?
  • How to reach doctor after hours?

Practices:

  • Do you meet before birth?
  • What’s your vaccination philosophy?
  • How do you handle school sports physicals?
  • Do you offer developmental screening?

Logistics:

  • Where do you admit patients?
  • What hospital do you use?
  • Do you have sibling policies?
  • How long are appointment slots?

Well-Child Visits Schedule
#

Recommended Schedule (AAP Guidelines)
#

Newborn: 3-5 days after birth
1 month: 1-month checkup
2 months: Vaccinations begin
4 months: 4-month checkup
6 months: 6-month checkup
9 months: 9-month checkup
12 months: 1-year checkup
15 months: 15-month visit
18 months: 18-month visit
24 months: 2-year checkup
30 months: 30-month visit
Annually: Yearly visits ages 3+

What Happens at Visits
#

Physical Exam:

  • Height and weight measurement
  • Head circumference (infants)
  • Vision and hearing screening
  • Developmental milestones

Vaccinations:

  • Given at recommended ages
  • Parent can ask questions
  • Side effects discussed
  • Record kept in vaccine card

Developmental Screening:

  • Age-appropriate milestones
  • Speech, motor, social skills
  • Concerns addressed
  • Referrals if needed

Parent Questions:

  • Sleep, feeding, behavior
  • Developmental concerns
  • Safety questions
  • Any other issues

Vaccinations
#

Standard Schedule
#

Infants:

  • Hepatitis B (birth)
  • DTaP (2, 4, 6 months)
  • Hib (2, 4, 6 months)
  • Polio (2, 4, 6 months)
  • PCV (2, 4, 6, 12-15 months)
  • Rotavirus (2, 4, 6 months)
  • MMR (12-15 months)
  • Varicella (12-15 months)
  • Hepatitis A (12-15 months, 2 doses)
  • Annual flu shot (age 6 months+)

Children:

  • Boosters at ages 4-6
  • Tdap at 11-12 years
  • HPV vaccine (11-12 years)
  • Meningococcal vaccine (11-12 years)

Vaccine Records
#

Keep Your Own:

  • Get vaccine card at each visit
  • Keep in safe place
  • Bring to new doctor if moving
  • School requires records

Office Records:

  • Office maintains records
  • Can request copies
  • State immunization registry may have records

Sick Visits
#

When to Call Doctor
#

Same-Day Visit Needed:

  • Fever over 100.4°F in infants under 3 months
  • High fever not responding to medication
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Severe pain
  • Signs of dehydration
  • Persistent vomiting or diarrhea
  • Rash with fever
  • Ear pain
  • Suspected infections

After-Hours Care:

  • Many offices have nurse advice line
  • After-hours phone service
  • Urgent care or ER if severe

Common Childhood Illnesses
#

Respiratory:

  • Colds and upper respiratory infections
  • Ear infections
  • Strep throat
  • Asthma episodes

Gastrointestinal:

  • Stomach flu
  • Vomiting and diarrhea
  • Constipation

Skin:

  • Rashes
  • Eczema
  • Infections

Emergency Care
#

When to Go to ER
#

Call 911 or Go to ER For:

  • Breathing difficulties
  • Severe injury
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Seizures
  • Severe allergic reaction
  • Poisoning or overdose
  • Head injury with concerning symptoms
  • Severe burns
  • Broken bone with deformity

Developmental Milestones
#

Important Ages
#

6 months: Sits without support, babbling
9 months: Crawling, saying “mama/dada”
12 months: Walking, first words
18 months: Walking well, 10+ words
2 years: Running, 50+ words, 2-word phrases
3 years: Riding tricycle, 3-word sentences

Developmental Concerns
#

Signs to Discuss:

  • Not meeting expected milestones
  • Loss of skills
  • Significant delays
  • Unusual behaviors

What Happens:

  • Further evaluation
  • Possible referrals
  • Early intervention services
  • Speech therapy, occupational therapy

Tips for Parents
#

  1. Choose carefully - Pediatrician relationship matters
  2. Keep records - Maintain vaccine and health records
  3. Ask questions - Don’t hesitate to ask about anything
  4. Be honest - Share all concerns with pediatrician
  5. Prepare for visits - Write questions ahead of time
  6. Know after-hours - Understand how to reach help
  7. Keep vaccinations current - Protect your child
  8. Watch milestones - Address concerns early
  9. Establish care early - Before baby arrives
  10. Trust your instincts - You know your child best
