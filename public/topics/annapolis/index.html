About Annapolis

History & Heritage

Annapolis serves as Maryland's capital city and is one of America's most historically significant communities. The city was designated Maryland's capital in 1695 and served as the first capital of the United States from 1783 to 1784, where the Treaty of Paris was ratified and George Washington resigned his commission as Commander-in-Chief.

The city's educational heritage dates back to 1696 with the founding of King William's School, which became St. John's College in 1784—making it the third oldest institution of higher education in the United States, after Harvard and William & Mary.

Geography & Location

Annapolis sits on the Chesapeake Bay at the mouth of the Severn River, approximately 25 miles south of Baltimore and 30 miles east of Washington, D.C. This strategic location has made it a center for maritime trade, naval education, and water sports for centuries.

The city is part of the Baltimore-Washington metropolitan area and benefits from proximity to two major economic centers while maintaining its distinctive small-city character.

Demographics

According to the 2020 U.S. Census, Annapolis has a population of 40,812 residents, representing a 6.3% increase from 2010. The city features a diverse racial and ethnic population with a well-educated community—significantly higher than national averages for educational attainment.

Quick Facts

  • Growth (2010-2020): +6.3%
  • Elevation: 10 feet above sea level
  • Area: 5.06 square miles

Top Attractions

Discover Annapolis' most significant historical sites, cultural attractions, and waterfront experiences.

U.S. Naval Academy

Founded in 1845, the U.S. Naval Academy is America's premier military service academy, training future leaders for the U.S. Navy and Marine Corps. The 544-acre campus features historic buildings, museums, and waterfront locations.

  • Founded: 1845
  • Location: 150 Blake Rd, Annapolis, MD 21402
  • Tours: Daily guided tours available
  • Admission: Free

Note: Visitors must register for tours in advance. Check official website for current requirements.

📍 View on Google Maps

Maryland State House

The Maryland State House is the oldest state capitol building in continuous legislative use in the United States. Built in 1772, it houses the Maryland General Assembly and features distinctive architecture including the largest dome of any state capitol building.

  • Built: 1772
  • Location: 100 State Circle, Annapolis, MD 21401
  • Tours: Free self-guided and guided tours
  • Hours: Monday-Friday, 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM
📍 View on Google Maps

Historic Downtown & Maryland Avenue

Annapolis' walkable downtown features preserved colonial architecture, boutique shopping, upscale dining, galleries, and historic sites. Maryland Avenue, the main shopping district, offers everything from antique stores to modern boutiques and restaurants.

  • Area: Historic District, Maryland Avenue
  • Features: Shopping, dining, galleries, historic sites
  • Parking: Multiple parking garages and street parking
  • Best Time to Visit: Weekdays for quieter experience

Annapolis Waterfront & Harbor

Known as "America's Sailing Capital," Annapolis waterfront features marinas, seafood restaurants, harbor cruises, and maritime museums. The city hosts the famous Annapolis to Bermuda Race and numerous sailing events throughout the year.

  • Features: Marinas, restaurants, cruise operators
  • Activities: Sailing, harbor cruises, seafood dining
  • Annual Events: Sailing races, boat shows
  • Best Season: May through October

Annapolis Neighborhoods

Explore the diverse residential and commercial neighborhoods that make up Annapolis.

Historic District

Character: Colonial architecture, walkable streets, waterfront access

The heart of Annapolis, featuring preserved 17th-19th century buildings, tree-lined streets, and proximity to the waterfront. Popular with professionals, retirees, and students.

  • Architectural Style: Colonial, Georgian, Federal
  • Walkability: High
  • Distance to Downtown: Walking distance

Eastport

Character: Waterfront living, yacht clubs, maritime atmosphere

Peninsula community with stunning harbor views, private docks, yacht clubs, and a relaxed maritime lifestyle. Popular with sailors and waterfront enthusiasts.

  • Features: Private docks, yacht clubs, harbor access
  • Atmosphere: Relaxed, maritime
  • Best For: Sailors, waterfront lovers

West Annapolis

Character: Historic estates, waterfront properties, larger lots

Luxury waterfront neighborhood with historic estates, large properties, and panoramic Chesapeake Bay views. One of the most prestigious areas in Maryland.

  • Features: Waterfront estates, large lots
  • Property Type: Luxury homes
  • Location: West side of Severn River

Eastport Heights

Character: Residential, family-friendly, good schools

Established residential neighborhood with tree-lined streets, good access to downtown, and highly-rated schools. Popular with families and young professionals.

  • Features: Single-family homes, parks
  • Schools: AACPS rated schools
  • Distance to Downtown: 10-15 minute drive

Schools & Education

Educational opportunities for Annapolis residents.

Public Schools

Annapolis residents attend Anne Arundel County Public Schools (AACPS), one of Maryland's largest school systems serving over 78,000 students. The district operates numerous elementary, middle, and high schools throughout the county.

Annapolis Area Schools

  • Elementary: Multiple AACPS elementary schools serve Annapolis
  • Middle School: Severn School or George S. Smith Middle School
  • High School: Annapolis High School (class of 2026 enrollment: ~450 students)

Higher Education

  • St. John's College: Founded 1696, unique liberal arts curriculum, two campuses (Annapolis and Santa Fe)
  • U.S. Naval Academy: Federal service academy, undergraduate education for naval officers

Note: School assignments may change. For the most current information, visit AACPS official website or contact the district directly.

Living in Annapolis

Cost of Living

Annapolis has a higher cost of living compared to national averages, driven primarily by housing costs. As Maryland's capital and a desirable waterfront city, real estate prices reflect the city's historical significance, maritime location, and proximity to Baltimore and Washington, D.C.

  • Housing: Above national average
  • Utilities: Near national average
  • Transportation: Below national average

Transportation

Annapolis offers multiple commuting options with convenient access to major employment centers. The city is served by MTA Maryland bus routes, with connections to Baltimore, Washington, D.C., and throughout Anne Arundel County.

  • Driving: MD-450, MD-284, US-50
  • Public Transit: MTA bus routes
  • Commuting to Baltimore: ~45 minutes via I-95
  • Commuting to Washington DC: ~60-75 minutes via I-95

Recreation & Activities

Annapolis residents enjoy extensive waterfront recreation, including sailing, kayaking, fishing, and boating. The city maintains several parks, marinas, and waterfront access points throughout the community.

  • Maritime Activities: Sailing, boating, kayaking
  • Parks: City parks and waterfront access
  • Dining: World-class seafood and restaurants
  • Cultural: Theater, music, historic sites

Healthcare

Annapolis and surrounding Anne Arundel County offer comprehensive healthcare services, including nearby hospital facilities in Annapolis and Glen Burnie.

  • Nearby Hospitals: Anne Arundel Medical Center (Glen Burnie)
  • Emergency Services: 24/7 emergency care available
  • Specialists: Multiple medical specialists in county
