Commuting & Transportation in Anne Arundel County#

Estimated reading time: 5 minutes

Living in Anne Arundel County means you’re between Baltimore and Washington DC, which is both a blessing and a curse for transportation. You’ve got options, but choosing the right ones depends entirely on where you work and how you want to spend your time.

Driving: The Default Option#

Let’s be honest - most people drive here. The county was built around cars, and even though public transit exists, driving is still the primary mode of transportation for the vast majority of residents.

Major Highways#

MD-2 Annapolis Boulevard: Connects Annapolis to the rest of the county. Can get congested during rush hour.

US-50: The main east-west artery. Runs through Glen Burnie, Severna Park, and Crofton. This is where you’ll see the most traffic during commute times.

MD-3: Runs north-south through the county. Connects to Baltimore via the Baltimore-Washington Parkway.

Baltimore-Washington Parkway: Connects directly to Baltimore and DC. The I-95 corridor runs nearby for interstate travel.

I-95: Accessible via exit 22 near Glen Burnie. This is your connection to the entire East Coast.

Traffic Reality#

Morning rush (6:30-9:00 AM): US-50 from Glen Burnie to Baltimore, and routes toward DC tend to be backed up. Expect 15-30 minutes of additional travel time.

Evening rush (4:00-7:00 PM): Reverse commute, with heavy traffic toward the county from DC and Baltimore.

Weekends: Generally free flowing, though weekend construction projects can cause unexpected delays.

Parking#

Work parking: Most employers in the county provide free parking. If they don’t, expect to pay $100-200/month in dedicated parking.

Street parking: In Annapolis and some downtown areas, expect to pay $1.50-3.00/hour during business hours. Residential areas typically require permits.

Public facilities: Most county facilities offer free parking, though popular locations like the state capital can get crowded on weekends.

Public Transit: What Actually Works#

MTA Maryland Commuter Bus#

The Maryland Transit Administration (MTA) runs commuter bus services from Anne Arundel County to DC and Baltimore.

Routes:

  • Route 4: Glen Burnie to DC (multiple stops along the way)
  • Route 12: Severna Park to Baltimore
  • Route 22: Crofton to DC

Cost:

  • Single ride: $2.00 cash, $1.50 with CharmCard
  • Monthly pass: $75 for unlimited rides on selected routes
  • Peak vs. off-peak pricing applies

Schedule:

  • Limited early morning and evening service
  • Reduced frequency on weekends
  • Check the MTA website for current schedules

Pros:

  • Predictable travel time (mostly on highway)
  • Can read/work during commute
  • No parking hassles

Cons:

  • Fixed schedules mean you need to plan around them
  • Can be crowded during peak hours
  • Not ideal for last-mile transportation once you arrive

MARC Train#

The MARC Penn Line runs from BWI Airport Station to Union Station in DC. This is only useful if you work in DC and live near BWI.

From BWI: You can take the Amtrak Marc train to Union Station in DC (about 45 minutes).

Cost:

  • Monthly pass: $150-250 depending on zone
  • Single ride: $5.75-12.00

Schedule:

  • Limited weekday service
  • No weekend service
  • Check schedules carefully before relying on this

Pros:

  • Fast to DC
  • More comfortable than bus
  • Can read/work during ride

Cons:

  • Only works for DC commuters
  • Limited schedule flexibility
  • Need to get to BWI first (parking or rideshare)

Ferry Services#

The Chesapeake Bay Bridge connects Annapolis to the Eastern Shore, but there are also ferry options for specific routes.

St. Leonard Ferry: Connects St. Leonard to Solomons Island. Free and runs 24/7. Useful if you live in southern Anne Arundel and work on the Eastern Shore.

Cost: Free

Schedule: Runs continuously, though wait times can vary during peak hours.

Rideshare and Taxis#

Uber and Lyft: Available throughout the county, but can be expensive for daily commuting. Better for occasional trips or when you need to get somewhere at odd hours.

Cost: Typically $15-40 per trip within the county, depending on distance and time of day.

Bike and Walk Options#

Anne Arundel is not a pedestrian-friendly county in most areas. Sidewalks and bike lanes are limited, and the road design prioritizes cars. However:

Annapolis: More walkable downtown area with some bike lanes.

Severna Park: Some sidewalks and bike paths, particularly near the water.

Crofton: Some bike paths and trails available.

Safety note: If you’re biking, use bike lanes where available and be very careful - car traffic is heavy and drivers aren’t always expecting cyclists.

BWI Airport Access#

Baltimore-Washington International Airport is right in Anne Arundel County, which is incredibly convenient for air travel.

Driving: 10-30 minutes depending on where you’re coming from in the county.

MARC train: BWI Rail Station connects to DC and Baltimore.

Rideshare: Available at the airport, though can be expensive during peak travel times.

My Perspective#

If you work in Baltimore or DC, public transit can work if you’re disciplined about schedules. But for most people in the county, driving is simply more flexible and practical.

The key is understanding your specific situation:

  • Where do you work?
  • How far is the commute?
  • Do you have children or other commitments that make flexible timing important?
  • Can you tolerate being stuck in traffic, or do you prefer a predictable schedule?

There’s no perfect answer, but knowing your options helps you make the right choice for your family.
