Living in Millersville, Maryland: Your Guide to Family-Focused Suburban Life

Complete guide to Millersville MD - family neighborhoods, top-rated schools, parks, housing, and why this Anne Arundel County community is perfect for families.

Welcome to Millersville, Maryland

Estimated reading time: 5 minutes

Your First Look at Millersville

Millersville is what Anne Arundel County does best when it comes to family living—suburban neighborhoods with top-rated schools, parks that actually get used, and a pace that lets childhood happen at a normal speed. The community has grown steadily over the years, and it shows in the infrastructure: wide streets, well-maintained parks, and a population that feels established but still growing.

Current population sits around 17,000-18,000 residents, which keeps things from feeling overwhelming while still supporting local businesses and community programs. If you’re looking for that sweet spot between “too small to have amenities” and “too big to know your neighbors,” Millersville hits it.

Where Millersville Fits in Anne Arundel County

Millersville is located in central Anne Arundel County, positioned:

  • North of: Gambrills and Odenton
  • South of: I-97 (the main north-south corridor)
  • West of: Severna Park
  • East of: Glen Burnie
  • To Annapolis: 15-20 minutes via I-97 or Route 171
  • To Baltimore: 20-25 minutes via I-97
  • To DC: 60-75 minutes depending on traffic
  • To BWI Airport: 15-20 minutes

The main access routes are I-97 (the Annapolis Boulevard) and MD Route 171, which connects Millersville to Annapolis. These are your primary arteries for commuting and getting around the county.

Schools: AACPS with Strong Reputation

Millersville is served by Anne Arundel County Public Schools (AACPS), and this is one of the area’s biggest draws. The schools have earned recognition for academic performance and community engagement.

Schools serving Millersville:

  • Elementary: Millersville Elementary School (on-site)
  • Middle: Odenton Middle School or nearby options
  • High: Severn High School or Annapolis High School depending on zone

The AACPS district has won 21 Maryland Blue Ribbon awards, and Millersville schools are part of that strong system. From parent discussions and school rating sites, the schools are well-regarded for:

  • Academic rigor
  • Parent involvement
  • Extracurricular programs
  • Support for military-connected children (proximity to Fort Meade helps)

The district has approximately 8,000 military-connected children across all schools, so military families will find the system accustomed to handling deployments and frequent moves.

Enrollment: Follows standard AACPS procedures. Millersville families use the county’s enrollment system like all AACPS residents.

Parks and Outdoor Spaces

Millersville has invested heavily in parks, and you can see the results in how actively residents use them.

Kinder Farm Park (288 acres):

  • Walking and biking trails through natural areas
  • Bird watching and nature observation
  • Open fields for casual play
  • Picnic areas
  • Large enough that it doesn’t feel crowded

Millersville Park (under construction as of 2024, expected completion summer 2025-2026):

  • 33-acre new community park across from Millersville Elementary
  • Tennis courts and pickleball courts
  • Athletic fields for organized sports
  • Playground facilities
  • Expected cost: approximately $12 million
  • Will host local athletic organizations for events

Marley Creek Park:

  • Located north of Elvaton Road
  • Provides green space for nearby residents
  • Natural creek and wooded areas

Regional parks within easy reach:

  • Quiet Waters Park (south toward Severna Park): 340 acres, trails, playgrounds, swimming
  • Jones Point Park (south toward Crownsville): Boat launch, trails, fishing
  • Sandy Point State Park (east toward Arnold): Beach, fishing, events

The park system is one of Millersville’s strengths—residents have options from large natural preserves to neighborhood playgrounds to organized sports facilities.

Housing: What to Expect

Millersville’s housing stock is primarily single-family homes in established neighborhoods, with some newer development.

Price ranges:

  • Median home value: Approximately $442,400 (as of recent data)
  • Typical range: $325,000 to $450,000+ depending on neighborhood and features
  • Median household income: Around $125,400

Neighborhoods:

Shipley’s Choice (Premier neighborhood):

  • Higher-end homes, larger colonials
  • Community pool
  • Strong school district access
  • True “community feel” with organized events
  • Higher price point

Gambrills area (adjacent to Millersville):

  • Family-friendly, well-established
  • Good schools and parks
  • Mix of home sizes and price points
  • Often grouped with Millersville in real estate searches

Other Millersville neighborhoods:

  • Primarily 1980s-2000s construction
  • Single-family homes on reasonable lot sizes
  • Established landscaping and mature trees
  • Mix of starter homes and larger family residences

The housing market here is competitive but not as heated as Annapolis waterfront. You’re paying for the school district and location, not waterfront premiums.

Shopping and Daily Needs

Millersville has local shopping within the community, with major retailers nearby:

Within Millersville:

  • Local grocery stores and pharmacies
  • Gas stations and convenience stores
  • Restaurants and casual dining
  • Retail strip centers

Nearby shopping:

  • Glen Burnie: 10-15 minutes west (Target, Costco, big-box retailers, outlet malls)
  • Odenton: 10 minutes north (shopping centers, restaurants, grocery)
  • Annapolis: 15-20 minutes east (City Dock specialty shopping, fine dining)
  • Severna Park: 10-15 minutes west (local shops, restaurants)

For major shopping trips, Glen Burnie is the primary destination—it has everything from big-box retailers to department stores. Annapolis handles the specialty shopping and dining experiences.

Healthcare Access

Nearby healthcare:

  • Anne Arundel Medical Center (Annapolis): Full-service hospital, 15-20 minutes
  • Urgent care: Multiple options in Glen Burnie, Odenton, and Severna Park
  • Primary care: Various physician practices throughout Millersville and surrounding areas

The healthcare infrastructure in this part of Anne Arundel County is solid. Primary care offices are local, and the full-service hospital is a reasonable drive for emergency or specialized care.

Commuting and Transportation

Driving: I-97 is your main commute route. It connects you to:

  • Annapolis (south)
  • Baltimore (north)
  • Route 171 provides an alternative route to Annapolis

During peak times, I-97 can get congested, especially the section between the Annapolis exit and the Baltimore area. Rush hour typically 6:30-9:00 AM and 4:00-6:30 PM.

Public transit: MTA Maryland bus routes serve the area, with connections to Annapolis, Glen Burnie, Baltimore, and ODOT (Odenton transit center).

To BWI Airport: 15-20 minutes via I-97—very convenient for travelers.

To Baltimore: 20-25 minutes via I-97 north.

To Annapolis: 15-20 minutes via I-97 south or Route 171.

To DC: 60-75 minutes via I-95 or Beltway connections.

The Millersville Vibe

Millersville works well if you want:

  • Family-focused suburban living
  • Top-rated public schools without private school costs
  • Access to parks and outdoor activities
  • Close proximity to both Annapolis and Baltimore
  • A community that feels established but still growing

It might not be ideal if you need:

  • Walkable town centers (you’ll drive for most things)
  • Urban amenities or nightlife (Annapolis is 15 minutes away)
  • Waterfront living (this is inland suburban)
  • Small-town isolation (it’s a developing suburban area)

What Makes Millersville Different

vs. Severna Park: Severna Park has stronger town-center identity and slightly older establishment. Millersville is more suburban-development focused with newer construction.

vs. Gambrills: Very similar—often grouped together. Millersville has better school access and more park acreage. Gambrills is closer to Route 2 and Annapolis.

vs. Odenton: Odenton is more military-proximate (closer to Fort Meade). Millersville is more traditional suburban with larger family homes.

vs. Glen Burnie: Glen Burnie is more urbanized with major retail and commercial. Millersville is more residential-focused with suburban character.

Practical Details

County services: All Anne Arundel County services apply—trash, recycling, police, fire, parks, libraries. Millersville residents use county facilities throughout the central county area.

Taxes: County-level property taxes only. No additional municipal taxes since Millersville is unincorporated.

Emergency services: Anne Arundel County Fire/Rescue and Police Department provide coverage. Response times are typical for suburban Maryland—generally good.

Libraries: Anne Arundel County Public Library serves Millersville. The Odenton Branch or Severna Park Branch are likely the nearest options.

Internet/utility coverage: Standard county infrastructure—electric, gas, water, sewer in all developed areas.

Population and Demographics

Current population: Approximately 17,000-18,000 residents

Median age: Around 39.8 years—typical family demographic with adults in their 30s-40s raising children

Median household income: Approximately $125,400—above the Maryland and national averages, reflecting the professional workforce and strong school district

Unemployment rate: Approximately 5.6% (within typical range for the region)

Political leanings: Voted Democratic in recent presidential elections, though this is a competitive Maryland suburban area

Future Development

Millersville is still developing. As of 2024-2026:

  • New Millersville Park under construction (33 acres)
  • Continued residential development in some areas
  • Infrastructure improvements to support growing population
  • Focus on family amenities and school capacity

The development pace is steady but not overwhelming, which should help maintain the community character as more families move in.

Bottom Line

Millersville is a solid choice for families who want suburban living with excellent public schools, good parks, and reasonable commutes to both Annapolis and Baltimore. The housing market is competitive but accessible compared to waterfront communities, and the community is focused on family amenities.

The trade-offs are typical for Maryland suburbs: drive for most things, suburban traffic during peak times, and you’re inland without waterfront. But if you value schools, parks, and family-focused living over waterfront access or walkability, Millersville delivers on those fundamentals.
