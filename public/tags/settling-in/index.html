Your First 30 Days in Anne Arundel County: Complete Checklist

Your First 30 Days in Anne Arundel County: Complete Checklist

Estimated reading time: 5 minutes

Moving to Anne Arundel County is a big step, and there’s a lot to keep track of in those first few weeks. This checklist will help you stay organized and make sure nothing falls through the cracks.

Week 1: The Essentials

Day 1-2: Arrival & Basic Setup

  • Find the nearest grocery store - Stock up on essentials: food, toiletries, cleaning supplies
  • Locate the nearest pharmacy - CVS, Walgreens, or local pharmacies throughout the county
  • Find the nearest urgent care clinic - Don’t wait until you need it
  • Test all home systems - Turn on water, check electrical outlets, test smoke detectors
  • Locate your trash and recycling pickup schedule - Contact landlord or check county website
  • Set up internet service - Call provider, schedule installation if needed
  • Start utility accounts - Electric (AEP), gas (if needed), water/sewer (if not included in rent)

Day 3-5: Official Documentation

  • Update driver’s license address - Visit Maryland MVA with required documents
  • Register your vehicle - Complete registration within 30 days
  • Update car insurance - Notify your provider of new address
  • Set up mail forwarding - Visit USPS office or go online
  • Update your address with employer - For payroll and tax purposes
  • Update address with banks - Credit cards, checking, savings accounts

Day 6-7: Healthcare Setup

  • Find a primary care physician - Call your insurance provider for in-network options
  • Transfer medical records - Request from previous doctor if needed
  • Register with a local pharmacy - Set up prescription transfers
  • Find a dental office - Especially important for families with children
  • Locate nearest hospital - Anne Arundel Medical Center or Calvert Memorial Hospital

Week 2: Community & Routine

Week 2 Tasks

  • Register to vote - Maryland resident registration available at mva.maryland.gov
  • Join community social media groups - Facebook groups for your specific area
  • Find a local gym or fitness center - County parks have pools and recreation facilities
  • Identify your local library branch - Free internet, computers, programs
  • Register children for school - Contact AACPS enrollment services
  • Find a pediatrician - If you have children, this is important
  • Schedule a family photo - Capture your new beginning in your new home

Week 2: Building Connections

  • Introduce yourself to neighbors - Build community relationships
  • Find a place of worship - If applicable to your beliefs
  • Join a recreational program - County recreation has activities for all ages
  • Subscribe to local newsletters - County or neighborhood newsletters
  • Locate emergency services - Know your precinct and fire district

Week 3: Deeper Integration

Week 3 Tasks

  • Explore your neighborhood - Walk or drive to understand the area
  • Find local parks and recreation areas - Check the county parks map
  • Identify your nearest fire station - Location for emergencies and snow emergencies
  • Set up routine maintenance - HVAC filter change, cleaning service if needed
  • Budget review - Assess your new cost of living situation
  • Update your resume/LinkedIn - Add new address for future opportunities
  • Register for county programs - Parks, recreation, senior services if applicable

Week 3: Financial Organization

  • Review all subscriptions - Cancel or transfer services as needed
  • Set up automatic bill payments - For utilities, mortgages, loans
  • Update emergency contacts - Local contacts, local doctor, local emergency services
  • Review insurance coverage - Ensure it matches Maryland requirements
  • Plan your commute - If working, test your commute during actual rush hour
  • Research local tax benefits - Maryland-specific deductions and credits

Week 4: Settling In & Future Planning

Week 4 Tasks

  • Complete your vehicle registration - Make sure this is done if not already
  • Schedule annual medical screenings - Doctor checkups, dental cleanings
  • Plan a family outing - Visit a local attraction together
  • Update all social media profiles - New location, new community
  • Research continuing education or professional development - Maryland has opportunities
  • Plan a housewarming or neighborhood gathering - If you want to build community
  • Review and adjust your budget - See how your new lifestyle is working

Making It Home

  • Decorate to feel comfortable - Add personal touches to make it feel like home
  • Find your coffee shop or favorite spot - Regular places build community
  • Attend a community event - County often hosts festivals and gatherings
  • Get to know local businesses - Support local economy and build connections
  • Create your local routine - Grocery shopping, gym, weekend activities

Important Contacts Summary

Emergency & Essential Services

  • Emergency: 911
  • Non-Emergency Police: 410-268-4444 (Annapolis), 410-268-4700 (Glen Burnie)
  • Fire Department: 410-266-4300 (Annapolis), 410-268-5100 (Glen Burnie)
  • Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222

Government & Administrative

  • Maryland MVA: 1-410-863-8000
  • Anne Arundel County: 1-410-222-3000
  • AACPS Enrollment: 410-222-8000
  • Maryland Department of Health: 1-877-404-1112

Utilities

  • AEP Maryland (Electric): 1-800-688-4673
  • Potomac Edison (Gas): 1-800-477-1004
  • Anne Arundel Water/Sewer: 410-222-3258

Common First-Month Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Not testing your commute during actual rush hour - Test it before finalizing work arrangements
  2. Waiting too long to get vehicle registered - Do it within the first two weeks
  3. Not setting up a primary care physician - Do this early, appointments can take time
  4. Ignoring local traffic patterns - Get to know the roads and parking situations
  5. Not joining community groups - Facebook groups, neighborhood associations
  6. Forgetting to update insurance - Maryland has specific requirements
  7. Not finding local services - Know where the nearest hospital, pharmacy, etc. are

Quick Reference: What You Need to Know

Vehicle Registration Fees

  • Standard passenger vehicle: $100-150
  • Motorcycle: $50-80
  • Additional fees may apply based on vehicle type

Maryland Driver’s License

  • Valid for 8 years
  • Requires vision test
  • Must surrender out-of-state license

Auto Insurance Requirements

  • 30/60/25 minimum coverage
  • $30,000 bodily injury per person
  • $60,000 bodily injury per accident
  • $25,000 property damage

Property Taxes

  • County-wide rate: varies by assessed value
  • Homestead tax credit available for primary residences
  • File with county Department of Tax and License Administration
